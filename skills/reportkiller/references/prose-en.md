# English prose: rules with evidence (all levels)

Source of the numbers: `levels.md` §3.1 (`levels.*.en`, `enRules`). Evidence: 研究ノート WRITING-EN (99 published texts, 821k words: A = leading journals, B = graduate/early-career, C = selected undergraduate work; AI side: HAP-E 1,227 academic pairs with GPT-4o and 18 Claude 5.5 drafts). Example sentences below are invented; they describe nothing real.

## 1. What separates levels (and what does not)

Mean sentence length does **not** separate levels (A 25.6, B 26.1, C 24.9 words). What does: **variation** (CV 0.47 vs 0.43; sentences under 10 words 6% vs 4%), **sentence-initial But** (2.3 vs 0.5 per 100 sentences; undergraduates use However instead), **stated thesis** in the introduction (63% vs 35%), **a question in the introduction** (63% vs 32%), **hedges** (6.4 vs 4.6 per 1,000 words; strong writers hedge more, to state the reach of evidence), em dashes, semicolons, colons. B is almost identical to A on the surface; what moves a text up is novelty and evidence, not style.

## 2. What separates AI drafts (and what does not)

GPT-4o: specific words at 30–400× human rates (multifaceted, intricate, pivotal, delve, underscore, tapestry, testament, cornerstone, "in conclusion"), uniform sentence lengths (SD 7 vs 15 words; 2% over 40 words vs 19%), sentence-final participial tails (", highlighting …" 8–14×), "not only … but" 12×, moreover/furthermore/additionally 4–9×, longer words (6.4 vs 5.5 letters), fewer passives, no "I", few quotations, few questions.

**Claude 5.5 drafts do not show those signs.** Their signs are different: 18–19% of sentences under 10 words (strong writers 6%), mean 20–22 words, headings 3.5–3.8 per 1,000 words (strong 1.1–1.4), introductions without a question, a gap or a roadmap, "rather than" 2.6–2.8× and "neither … nor" 2–3×, "X is not A. It is B." reframing (Opus 9×), First/Second/Third enumeration, no em dashes. Because ReportKiller drafts in Claude, **the Claude signs matter more than the GPT-4o word list.**

Not evidence of AI (do not "fix"): passive voice (AI uses *less*), "it is important to note" (same rate as humans), "This shows …" openers, paragraph-final summary sentences, em dashes (strong writers use about 3 per 1,000 words; banning them moves text away from the best writing).

## 3. Rules R1–R24 (short form; ug = from undergraduate, grad = from graduate)

- **R1 State the thesis with a claim verb** (I argue / This Article shows) within the introduction; a purpose sentence ("This paper explores …") is not a thesis. (all; strict from ug)
- **R2 Open with a concrete case or the received view**, not "Throughout history …", "In today's world …", a superlative, or a dictionary definition. (all)
- **R3 They say → but → I say → roadmap.** Name the received view, say specifically what it fails to measure or explain, then state the claim. "More research is needed" is not a gap. (ug; required from grad)
- **R4 Roadmap for texts over 5,000 words** ("Part I … Part II …" or "The argument proceeds in three steps"). (grad)
- **R5 Mix sentence lengths.** Short sentences (under 12 words) at turns and conclusions, long sentences (over 35) where reasons and conditions belong together. CV ≥ 0.40 (ug 0.35). Do not ban sentences over 40 words (strong writing 5–20%). (all)
- **R6 One point per paragraph, stated in the first sentence** (then/thus may link to the previous paragraph). 4–6 sentences, 80–190 words typical; paragraphs of 8+ sentences ≤ 45%; one-sentence paragraphs ≤ 10%. (all)
- **R7 Old information first, new information last**; subject then verb; actions as verbs, not nominalisations; "This" followed by a noun. (all)
- **R8 Short connectives.** But, Yet, Thus, Indeed, Instead, Rather. Sentence-initial But is fine (strong writers: 2–3 per 100 sentences). Put about half of "however" mid-sentence. Moreover/furthermore/additionally rarely, never two in a row. Never "In conclusion". (all)
- **R9 Match claim strength to evidence.** May/might/could/likely/suggest where evidence is partial; say in the same paragraph what cannot be concluded. No three hedges in one sentence. Clearly/obviously/undoubtedly/prove only with evidence in that or the previous sentence. (ug)
- **R10 First person for method and claim** ("I argue", "I turn now to", "We estimate"); field norms vary (philosophy 6, economics 3, politics/sociology 1.3, history 0.9, law 0.6 per 1,000 words). (ug)
- **R11 Choose voice by function.** Active when the actor is the point; passive to keep the topic in subject position or where the actor is unknown. Do not reduce passives as a rule (AI already uses fewer). Review above 35% passive sentences. (all)
- **R12 Quote short, embed, then explain.** Paraphrase by default; quote only when the wording is the point; quotations ≤ 15% of words; after a quotation, say what it shows. (all)
- **R13 Prior work by issue, attributed by name.** One view per sentence, then "But/Yet" with its specific limitation. "Many scholars argue" needs a citation in the same sentence. (ug)
- **R14 The strongest objection, stated fairly, answered with evidence.** "One might object that …", "To be sure, …", "It is true that … But …"; answer in 1–2 sentences by showing error, weighing, or narrowing the claim. Do not label the move ("A counterargument to this is …") or list objections like bullets. Two or more objections from grad. (ug)
- **R15 Conclusion: restate in new words, say what changes, say the limits.** No "In conclusion"; word overlap with the opening below 0.2; no new points. (all)
- **R16 Same term for the same concept.** No elegant variation. (all)
- **R17 Use colons, semicolons and em dashes** (em dashes up to 7 per 1,000 words). Questions in the introduction and at section openings are welcome. (ug)
- **R18 No reflexive triplets or contrast templates.** "A, B, and C" only when there are three items with content; "not only … but also" ≤ 1 per 2,000 words; "not X but Y" ≤ 1 per 1,000. (all)
- **R19 No sentence-final participial tails** (", highlighting the importance of …"). Put the interpretation in a sentence with a subject. (all)
- **R20 Plain, specific words.** No tier-1 words (§5). Mean word length ≤ 5.9 letters. Say why something matters instead of calling it pivotal. (all)
- **R21 Argue in paragraphs.** Headings ≤ 2 per 1,000 words (ug 3); no bullets or bold in the body. (all)
- **R22 Moving up is substance, not surface.** From grad: novelty, non-obviousness, usefulness stated in the introduction; a limitations paragraph; two or more objections. (grad)
- **R23 Do not overuse short sentences** (Claude habit). Under-10-word sentences ≤ 14% (ug 15%); if the mean falls under 20 words, join sentences so that reasons and conditions share a sentence. (all)
- **R24 Do not make logical-template phrases a habit** (Claude habit). "rather than" ≤ 1 per 1,000 words; "neither … nor", "The problem is not X. It is Y.", "First, … Second, … Third," and "three reasons" each only once or twice per document. (all)

## 5. Word tiers (研究ノート WRITING-EN §5.4)

- **Tier 1 — do not use in generated text**: delve, underscore, showcase/showcasing, tapestry, testament, cornerstone, multifaceted, intricate/intricacies, pivotal, paramount, transformative, unravel, pave the way, realm, endeavor, invaluable, meticulous(ly), commendable, seamless(ly), harness, navigate (figurative), interplay, advancements, boasts, groundbreaking, vibrant, ever-evolving, "a deeper understanding", in conclusion / in summary. Replace with the plain word or with the content (what changed, why it matters).
- **Tier 2 — at most once per document**: nuanced, nuance(s), bolster, profound(ly), foster, leverage, vital, embrace, tangible, garner, grapple, resonate, myriad, the intersection of, aligns with, holistic, innovative, undoubtedly.
- **Tier 3 — watch the density** (strong writing median 2.1 per 1,000, 95th percentile 5.7): crucial, significant (non-statistical), insights, landscape, enhance, robust, ultimately, moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably, serves as, highlight(s), framework, potential, findings.

## 6. How not to use this

These checks are for revising the skill's own draft. They are not a detector of machine authorship: detectors misjudge non-native writers (Liang et al. 2023), 4% of undergraduate windows hit two signals, and every sign above also occurs in strong human writing. Report the property ("sentence lengths are uniform"), never "this sounds like AI". Never act on a single signal.
